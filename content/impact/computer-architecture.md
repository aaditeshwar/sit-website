## The problem

AI workloads, phones, data centres and tiny edge devices must all deliver more performance within tight power and thermal budgets, and data movement through memory often costs more energy than the computation itself.

This cannot be solved in the lab alone. Solutions have to fit real chips and real products.

The security side of this work is [Cybersecurity](/impact/cybersecurity).

## What we do

The **Memory and embedded Architecture Research Group (MARG)** works on energy-efficient embedded systems, shared-cache management, hardware for AI/ML, application-specific accelerators, emerging 3D and non-volatile memory, electronic design automation, and high-level and system-level synthesis. Related work spans thermal-aware systems, **ML-driven industrial and PCB design**, **AR/VR design tools**, neuromorphic hardware for **very low-power navigation on drones**, hardware for AI on the chip (an R-Systems CoE project), and **quantum-circuit compilation**.

## How it grew

::timeline

This story is not about a single product. It is about **a way of working with industry**. Projects begin with an industrial problem and a partner who can take the result into products. Colleagues then build lasting groups and centres around them.

## Impact

::metrics

- **Industry-sponsored research:** Samsung (smartphone energy efficiency), Cadence (edge ML algorithms and architectures), the Semiconductor Research Corporation (multiprocessor memory hierarchies), and research collaborations with Intel and NXP.
- **Recognition:** best-paper nominations at DATE and CODES+ISSS (2022), the VLSI Design/Embedded Systems Best PhD Thesis Award (2019), the FITT Award for an industry-relevant thesis, first prize at the Cadence Tensilica hackathon (VLSID 2019), the IESA Techno-mentor Award (2019), and Prime Minister's Research Fellowships for SIT scholars.
- **Field leadership:** SIT faculty serve as editor-in-chief of *IEEE Embedded Systems Letters*, associate editors of *IEEE TCAD*, and programme and general chairs of CASES and the VLSI Design conference.
- **People:** SIT-trained PhDs and M.Tech. graduates now work in the semiconductor industry, including at Intel. <!-- TODO: add an alumni count or examples -->

## What's next

New directions include **energy-efficient on-chip AI accelerators**, **AI for chip and PCB design**, **neuromorphic computing** for edge robotics, and **quantum transpilation**. Most are open to PhD, MS (Research) and pre-doctoral researchers.
