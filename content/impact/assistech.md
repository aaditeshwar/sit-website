## The problem

India has one of the world's largest populations of blind and visually impaired people. For most of them, two things decide independence: **moving around safely** and **getting an education**. The white cane only detects obstacles below the knee and within about a metre, so overhanging branches, parked trucks and signboards are hazards. Braille displays and tactile diagrams for science and maths can cost more than a family earns in months. As Assistech's founders put it, the real barrier in low-income countries is often not *whether* a technology exists, but **whether it is affordable**.

## What we built

**Assistech** is an interdisciplinary group of faculty, research staff and students, based in SIT. Its members include computer scientists, electrical and mechanical engineers, product designers and visually impaired users. It works across four areas: **research and development, translational research, user engagement, and awareness and diffusion.**

**Mobility**
- **SmartCane:** an ultrasonic obstacle detector that clips onto a standard folding white cane. It warns through vibration of obstacles from knee to head height up to three metres away. Repeated user trials reshaped its design, including the grip and handle size; the final validation involved more than 150 users in six cities. SmartCane Version 2 is now in large-scale trials.
- **OnBoard:** tells a blind commuter the route number of an approaching bus. It was trialled on 25 buses on two routes with BEST in Mumbai, and operator feedback led to a directional antenna design.
- **MAVI** (Mobility Assistant for the Visually Impaired): AI for Indian streets, reading multilingual hand-painted signs and warning of potholes and animals.
- **IncluNav:** indoor navigation with smartphone apps and Bluetooth beacons.

**Education and information access**
- **DotBook:** an affordable **refreshable braille display** built on proprietary shape-memory-alloy braille cells, launched in 2019. A new refreshable braille display co-created with Saksham Trust is now in trials.
- **Tactile graphics:** a software flow plus 3D-printed moulds that cut the cost of raised-line diagrams for STEM textbooks by at least five times. It is housed in a **Centre of Excellence in Tactile Graphics** at IIT Delhi.
- **RAVI** (Reading Assistant for the Visually Impaired): converts PDFs, including **equations, tables and diagrams**, into formats that screen readers can handle.
- **TacRead** and other tactile interfaces for reading digital text, and STEM learning kits such as the *Shapescapes* geometry kit.

## How it grew

::timeline

Assistech's journey is SIT's model in action. **A course project** with a clear user need, sharpened by the Saksham Trust, became SmartCane. **Several faculty took ownership** of a broader portfolio of mobility and education tools, with industrial and philanthropic partners to manufacture and distribute them. The group then became **a hub for the national assistive-technology ecosystem**. Its work includes spinning out the Raised Lines Foundation, hosting the ICMR-supported NCAHT, sending revenue back into research and convening conferences.

## Impact

::metrics

- **SmartCane is used by about 150,000 people** in India and abroad. It is distributed through organisations for blind people, together with the training needed to use it, and is available under the Government of India's ADIP scheme.
- **Tactile STEM books** from the Raised Lines Foundation have reached thousands of blind students across India.
- **National capacity:** in June 2024 the ICMR Director General launched new products from NCAHT at IIT Delhi, including a geometry learning kit, high-quality white canes and an accessible STEM education kit, alongside large-scale trials of SmartCane Version 2 and a new refreshable braille display.
- **Recognition:** co-founder Prof. M. Balakrishnan received the **2018 ACM Eugene L. Lawler Award for Humanitarian Contributions within Computer Science**, and two national awards for assistive technology. Assistech's work has been covered by *The Wall Street Journal*, *Reuters*, *The Economic Times* and *The Hindu*, among others.

## What's next

The team's priorities are AI-based mobility assistance (MAVI), accessible indoor navigation (a current R-Systems CoE project), generative AI for accessing educational material, and low-cost braille displays at volume. All of them are open to students and collaborators.
