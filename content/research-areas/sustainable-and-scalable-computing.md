As AI becomes part of every product and service, the energy and cost of computation become a sustainability problem in their own right. This area rethinks the full stack, **from new materials and chip architectures to operating systems and ML workflows**, so that computing scales without costing the planet.

## Featured work

### Systems for sustainable machine learning

With large, accurate ML models widely available and AI applications booming, much of the ML workload in data centres has shifted from training to inference. Optimising inference is therefore central to green AI. Ongoing research improves the latency and throughput of ML inference serving systems, and builds cost-effective and sustainable ML workflows such as [Popper](http://abhilash-jindal.com/assets/popper).

![Sustainable ML systems](/images/research/sustainable-and-scalable-computing-1.png)

**Faculty:** [Abhilash Jindal](https://abhilash-jindal.com/), [Rijurekha Sen](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~rijurekha/), [Kaustubh Beedkar](https://web.iitd.ac.in/~kbeedkar/)

### Embedded systems and system-on-chip architecture

AI/ML applications, emerging hardware platforms and new fabrication and packaging technologies are converging. This creates opportunities for higher performance and more compact systems, along with hard design challenges. Performance must respect tight power and energy budgets. Large volumes of data must be orchestrated through future memory systems, and computation must be partitioned and mapped for optimal utilisation, with application-specific accelerators meeting the throughput and latency needs of modern workloads. Research here spans next-generation memory systems, AI/ML hardware and 3D chip stacking.

![SoC architecture](/images/research/sustainable-and-scalable-computing-2.png)

**Lead faculty:** [Preeti Ranjan Panda](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~panda/) · [MARG](https://marg.iitd.ac.in/)

### Biomaterial-based computing

Silicon is still the workhorse of commercial nanotechnology, yet nature uses proteins to perform a dazzling variety of roles at the nanoscale. This research aims to **fabricate computing devices from biological protein polymers**, starting with microtubules. Microtubules are mechanically robust and abundant in cells, and a lattice-like arrangement of aromatic residues allows surprisingly efficient electronic energy migration. Building on DNA-computing approaches, the work investigates how photochemical information in microtubules can be read, stored and converted for pattern-based computing such as cellular automata. It uses fluorescence microscopy and ultrafast spectroscopy, and explores protein polymers for memory, light harvesting and energy transduction, as a contribution to green computing.

![Microtubule-based automata](/images/research/sustainable-and-scalable-computing-3.png)

**Faculty:** [Aarat Kalra](https://www.kalralab.org/)

### ML-based technologies for industrial design

Industrial design of 3D models and PCBs still relies on slow manual tools with a steep learning curve. This work uses fast generative-AI algorithms, in the spirit of coding copilots, to generate engineering designs automatically, starting with a PCB design tool. GPU-based simulation of complex physical phenomena helps the AI-driven optimisation converge quickly.

![ML for industrial design](/images/research/sustainable-and-scalable-computing-4.jpg)

**Lead faculty:** [Smruti R. Sarangi](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~srsarangi)

### AR/VR for Industry 4.0

PCBs, 3D designs and IIoT components are still designed with a mouse, a keyboard and a 2D screen. Modern AR, VR and haptics make it possible to **design 3D in 3D**. This project is creating a new kind of 3D design tool that combines these interfaces with generative AI and LLMs.

![AR/VR for Industry 4.0](/images/research/sustainable-and-scalable-computing-5.jpg)

**Lead faculty:** [Smruti R. Sarangi](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~srsarangi)
