Good healthcare depends as much on reaching people as on medical science. Work in this area builds **technology that widens access to care**. It includes assistive devices for persons with disabilities, AI that helps clinicians detect cancer earlier, and analytics that show where public-health programmes are falling short. Much of it is developed hand in hand with partners such as AIIMS Delhi and PGI Chandigarh.

## Featured work

### Assistive Technologies Laboratory (Assistech)

Assistech researches and develops assistive technology solutions that improve the lives of persons with disabilities, using embedded systems, AI/ML, robotics and product design. The goal is to open up education, independent and safe living, and employment for people with visual impairment, older people, children with disabilities, and people with speech and hearing impairments. Active areas include safe and accurate navigation for visually impaired people, AI-based robotic assistants for older people and those who need nursing care, deep-learning text-to-speech, generative AI for accessing educational material, devices that help children with disabilities attend classes and take exams, low-cost tactile braille displays, embedded AI that ensures assistive devices are used correctly, and tools for tactile graphics.

### Immunisation uptake modelling and data collation

Vaccines are among humankind's greatest public-health technologies, but deploying them raises behavioural and social challenges. The WHO now lists vaccine hesitancy as a key public-health risk. This work integrates survey and administrative datasets to estimate under-vaccination locally, using geospatial machine learning to cope with missing and uneven data. It also models vaccination outcomes as a function of supply-side and demand-side factors, linked to a broad range of health and socio-economic indicators. Doing so requires new statistical computing methods that combine multilevel, hierarchical and latent-variable models.

![Immunisation modelling](/images/research/health-and-social-care-technologies-1.jpg)

**Faculty:** [Sumeet Agarwal](https://web.iitd.ac.in/~sumeet/), [Aaditeshwar Seth](https://www.cse.iitd.ac.in/~aseth/) · **Collaborators:** Mira Johri (University of Montreal), Alex de Figueiredo (London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine)
